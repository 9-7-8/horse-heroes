# Failure and stakes

What happens when a journey goes wrong. The rule itself is the owner's and is
stated first. The details after it are recommendations, with prototype
numbers.

> Failure should protect affection while creating a useful next decision:
> "Everyone is safe. We brought something back. We know what to change."

## Confirmed by the owner

1. **Horses and pets cannot die.**
2. When the party's health and its usable health potions are exhausted, the
   party **returns to the previous city**.
3. It **loses a configurable amount of loot**.
4. It **pays a modest cost** in money or materials.
5. It then **waits for the player to send it out**. It **never automatically
   retries** the failed route.

## Principles

### The promise

A failed journey means "we need a different plan", never "we lost our
friends". Companions, pets, earned identity, looks and real world progress
are preserved. A limited economic setback makes preparation matter **without
making experimentation frightening**.

### What to take from other games, and what to refuse

- **Take:** defeat that is not death; a safe place to recover; the team kept
  whole. A rescue fiction and a bounded setback. Clear recovery thresholds,
  awareness of supplies, and an explanation of why an unattended build was
  unsafe.
- **Refuse:** character death; permanent loss of equipped items; random
  removal of a weapon, an heirloom or protected cargo; hardcore stakes.

This game is gentler than any model in which equipment is destroyed.

### When retreat happens

- A horse at zero health is **exhausted** and stops acting. It is not dead.
- **One exhausted horse does not end the expedition.** The others can finish
  the fight. A healthy horse waiting on a cooldown is not a defeated party.
- A pet may become temporarily unavailable if pets ever have health. **Do
  not invent pet health just to punish it.**
- Potions are used automatically, under explicit rules for whom and how
  often. Recommended: a potion can restore an exhausted horse. No separate
  revival currency and no retrieval errand.
- Automatic potions do not promise safety. A large burst can still overwhelm,
  and **that attack must be readable** in the preview and the report.
- Retreat resolves when no horse can fight and no usable recovery can restore
  one. A potion that cannot be used does not keep a beaten party fighting.

### Retreat is a resolved outcome

    travelling -> encounter -> defeated -> retreat resolved
      -> safe in the departure city -> waiting for the player

1. Combat and all further progress stop.
2. Rewards already validly earned are kept. A boss that was not beaten pays
   no completion reward.
3. The loot loss is worked out, **once**.
4. The recovery charge is worked out, **once**.
5. The party returns to the city it left from.
6. Health is restored as part of recovery. No long healing timer.
7. A report is shown.
8. Any queue of repeat journeys is cleared. The party waits.

**No ambush on the way back, and no fee per tile.** The retreat is not
another dangerous journey.

### What can be lost

Recommended: only **unprotected equipment picked up on this failed trip** and
still in shared cargo. That protects unsold shop stock and spare gear brought
from earlier.

Never lost: horses or pets; equipped gear, saddlebags and pet gear included;
locked or favourite gear; anything a quest depends on; protected loadout
pieces; unlocked looks and dyes; family history, relationships, restored
towns.

Unused potions come home. Used ones stay used.

### How much

- Recommended preset: a tenth of the eligible items by count, rounded down,
  and at most three per failure.
- **Lowest rarity first**, then a stable tie-break. Never a rare piece
  because of the order drops happened to spawn in. The selection rule is
  shown beside the percentage.
- Settings: the percentage (including none), the cap, and an optional
  "protect Rare or better".
- Rounding is explained. A small failed trip may lose nothing, and that is
  fine: time, spent potions and the charge are already a cost.
- **Locking must never become a chore.** A "protect rare finds" rule saves
  attention. If a player protects everything, respect it; do not invent a
  substitute penalty.
- No pile left on the map and no backtracking to fetch it. A later recovery
  service must never be needed for the rule to be fair.

### A modest cost

- **One channel:** coins, or an existing material. Not both by default.
  Coins are simplest and tie into the caravan shop.
- Suggested: the least of a small charge for the route's tier, a twentieth of
  the wallet, and a fixed cap.
- **Never more than the party has.** An empty wallet still recovers: no debt,
  no blocked departure, no forced sale.
- In the fiction it is help at a waystation, restocking shared supplies,
  help packing the caravan. Not payment for rescuing friends from death.
- **Never a currency that exists only for failure.**
- Spent potions are already a cost, so tune the charge low. It should mark a
  setback, not undo several good town visits.

### Options, not an "easy mode"

Defaults: nobody dies; nothing equipped is lost; a small loss of the trip's
surplus; a small capped charge; immediate safe recovery; relaunch by hand.

Offer the percentage, rare protection and the scale of the charge as
independent settings, explained before departure, and let players turn the
economic losses off. **Do not label any of it as a lesser mode.**

**Add nothing on top:** no timed penalties, injuries, relationship decay or
lost reputation. Those would quietly undo the rule.

### Away from the screen

- **The first failure ends the expedition and every automatic repeat.**
  Unattended time is resolved only up to that retreat. The rest of the time
  away produces no more fights, no more potions spent, no more charges.
- A new journey starts only when the player sends the party. **Changing a
  loadout or opening the map is not consent to retry.**
- If the stall opens by itself on return, that is its own explicit setting.
  Nothing rare is listed and nothing sold just because the party failed.
- Each expedition has a unique ID and a "failure resolved" mark. Recovery,
  loss, charge and return are committed together, so reopening a save can
  never apply the penalty twice or repeat a reward. **Changing a setting
  never rerolls a loss already resolved.**

### A report that teaches

It answers: where did we stop; what beat us; did the potions run out or was
the burst too large; which horse, element or defence mattered; what did we
keep; exactly what was lost and charged; what could we change.

**A suggestion must come from the simulation's data.** If the cause is
uncertain, show the damage types and the potions used. Never a guessed
diagnosis.

### Tone

- The words are **Exhausted**, **Retreated**, **Returned safely**. Never the
  language of death.
- Horses regroup, pets settle beside them, the return is calm. **No corpses,
  and no cute animation of a pet dying.**
- Sound moves from combat to relief. Losses are listed plainly: no alarm and
  no humiliating banner. Health visibly comes back.
- It is still a story beat: the gaggle tried, learned and came home
  together. It need not be as bright as a victory to be gentle.

### World progress

- **Failure never re-corrupts a restored place.** Valid discoveries and
  completed milestones are kept. A boss not beaten remains.
- Experience already earned stays. Do not stack an experience loss on loot,
  coins, time and potions.
- Turning back **by choice**, between encounters, is a separate thing.
  Decide whether it costs nothing or a small known amount. Caution is not
  defeat.

## For Horse Heroes

- **This is an exception to idle-first, and it is the owner's.** Pillar 2
  says the game progresses with no input; after a failure it deliberately
  does not. The reasoning is sound for an idle game: an unattended party that
  retries a route it cannot beat would burn potions and coins while nobody
  watches. The pillar still governs everything that is going well.
- The slice already treats a lost trail gently: it ends and pays less.
  Nothing dies. What is new is the return to a city, the loss, the charge and
  the wait.
- **Potions do not exist yet.** The rule depends on them, so they are a
  mechanic to build, and the fairy spring and the recovery ideas already
  filed are the natural place to start.
- A horse "falling" in the slice is the exhaustion state under another name.
  The death events planned for combat feedback apply to enemies; a horse gets
  an exhausted pose.
- "Lowest rarity first" for loss mirrors "highest rarity first" for keeping
  ([cargo.md](cargo.md)). One ordering serves both, read from opposite ends.
- The report here is the trail report already filed, with a failure's
  questions added. One report, not two.
- "Do not label it a lesser mode" matters for the settings screen: these sit
  beside the feel settings as preferences, not under a difficulty heading.
- The seeded, deterministic sim is what makes "resolve once, never reroll"
  checkable.
