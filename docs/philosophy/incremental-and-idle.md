# Incremental and idle

What accumulation, reinvestment and absence need in order to be a game and not
a number treadmill.

## Principles

### Incremental: accumulation and reinvestment

    act -> receive a resource -> spend it on improvement
      -> act more efficiently -> receive more

**Clear cause and effect.** The player must be able to tell what produces each
resource, what spends it, what an upgrade changes, why output rose or fell,
what the next useful decision is, and whether a choice is permanent,
reversible or risky. Not every formula has to be shown, only enough for an
informed choice. "Income increased by 1.7%" is bad feedback. Naming the thing,
the new rate, the bottleneck and the payback time is good feedback.

**Quick early rewards, real later decisions.** The opening teaches the loop
fast: first resource, first upgrade, first visible gain, first automation,
first moment the game carries on by itself. After that, progress must shift
from speed to choice.

> Keep changing the question, not merely enlarging the answer.

Buying the same upgrade again for a larger number is the weak curve. The
strong one unlocks a resource, which opens a second path, which creates a
trade-off, which a new challenge then tests.

**Reinvestment must show.** An upgrade that exists only in a menu did not
happen. Something appears, grows, fires more, wears new armour, clears faster,
or beats what used to block it. In an idle RPG the best evidence is behaviour:
"my party now survives the enemy that killed it".

**A new layer must deepen the loop.** It should create a decision, solve a
limitation, create a synergy or a strategy, set a goal, give an old resource
new meaning, or enrich the fantasy. Never add a layer that is only another
currency with another button.

- Warning sign: the player has a resource and no interesting decision about
  it.
- The question to ask: **what tension does this resource introduce?** (Spend
  now or save for something expensive; safe farming or a risky hunt.)
- Reuse a currency where that creates tension. Name resources by purpose.
  Keep each one's source and sink visible. Never create one only to gate a
  menu.

**The next goal is visible.** The player should rarely wonder what to care
about. Surface a short list of near-term goals, without forcing one path.

> Show the mountain, but do not force only one trail.

### Idle: respecting absence

> The game respects time when the player is absent.

The player returns to progress, never to punishment for leaving.

    Idle play creates progress.
    Active play creates insight, agency and optional optimisation.

**Absence must be explained.** Do not just show a total. A return report says
how long, how many runs, how many failed and the main cause, what was earned,
what was notable, what the current bottleneck is, and a suggested response.
That turns absence into a diagnosis: the player comes back to an interesting
problem.

**The player authors the automation.** If the game decides everything, it is
passive watching. If nothing happens without input, it is not idle. The player
configures enough to own the outcome: where, how hard, with what build, what
to keep and what to scrap, when to stop. Those settings should be visible in
the report, so results can be traced to them.

**Active play improves quality; it is never mandatory labour.** Watching may
give faster runs, better choices at rare moments, a riskier reward path. It
must never require constant tapping for normal income, claim buttons with no
decision, logging in every few minutes, collecting ordinary drops by hand, or
scrapping common items one click at a time.

**Waiting needs anticipation, not helplessness.** Good waiting has something
coming: a boss attempt, a threshold, a set one piece short. Bad waiting leaves
nothing to plan, inspect or compare, and closing the game is the only sensible
move. Never block the player with a timer after every decision is made.

**Offline gains need boundaries.** A cap on time away, a run limit, a safe
profile that pays less and rarely fails, reduced reward for unattended risky
content, a stop after repeated failures, and a simulation the player can
understand. The purpose is not to punish absence but to keep a reason to come
back and decide something.

**Do not demand checking.** No decay that erases progress during absence.
Returns are valuable because of the decision waiting there.

### Prestige

A reset must answer: why would I trade current progress for a new beginning?

- Bad: "reset because the spreadsheet says it is efficient".
- Good: the old run becomes a legacy whose discoveries give the next one new
  possibilities.

Give it a place in the fiction. Keep continuity (the codex, discovered powers,
templates, cosmetics, bounded permanent choices). Make the next cycle visibly
different. Explain the gain before the reset, and let the player choose when.
It should feel like beginning a richer cycle, not deleting work.

## For Horse Heroes

- The incremental loop is not closed: coins are earned and cannot be spent.
  Coins are today exactly the warning sign above. Their sink must come with a
  tension, not just a price list.
- Losing a trail gives no reason. A win-or-lose result with no cause is the
  "7,402,918 gold" report in miniature. Explaining a loss is worth more than
  a new system.
- Camp is the wait. Its 12 seconds should be spent anticipating or deciding,
  and when there is nothing to decide the player can already skip it. Keep
  that true as camp fills up: no timer may block a player who has decided.
- Nothing is saved, so there is no absence to respect yet. Offline progress
  starts with saving.
- The sim is deterministic and free of rendering, so an offline run can be the
  real fight, not an estimate. That is the "clear simulation model" for free.
- Every new system gets the tension question in its issue. If a resource has
  no tension, reuse an existing one.
- A premise for prestige is available without inventing one: the party comes
  home, and what it learned stays in the stable. Not designed; noted so that
  the reset, if it comes, is a homecoming and not a deletion.
