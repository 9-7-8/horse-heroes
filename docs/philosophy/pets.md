# Pets

Pets are confirmed: collectible companions assigned to individual horses, who
ride on saddles ([vision.md](vision.md)). Whether they do anything in play is
still the owner's decision. This file is the recommendation she was given for
that decision, kept as guidance for when it is made.

> Cute companions with a small, distinct gameplay role, not extra party
> members whose gear and abilities double the build-management workload.

## Principles

### A pet enriches decisions the player already makes

The loop: find or adopt a pet on the road or in town; choose which horse it
goes with; give it a collar; it contributes one signature behaviour by itself;
back in town, change the pet, its collar or its horse.

**No separate full build screen.** A pet adds to party composition and gear
choices. It does not open a second game beside them.

### One recognisable job each

A pet has one passive and one signature ability, automatic and easy to
understand. **Its value should be identifiable from its behaviour, not buried
in a list of tiny bonuses.**

Example jobs, not a required roster:

| Job | What it does | Why the party wants it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scout | Spots ambushes, picks out elite enemies | Preparing for danger |
| Forager | Finds a little useful material on a journey | Exploration |
| Warden | Now and then shields its horse | That horse's survival |
| Elemental friend | Applies a light setup status | Opening a reaction |
| Mender | Restores a little health after an encounter | Lasting a long trip |

Start with two or three.

### Three levels of identity

- The horse's **class** defines how it fights.
- The horse's **element** defines its elemental contribution.
- The **pet** defines one more kind of support.

A pet is never a fourth class or a second damage system. An elemental pet
creates an opening; the horses supply the payoff.

**Pet effects have clear limits and never chain** into further pet or horse
effects without bound. Their contribution must be easy to see in combat.

### Gear: a collar, and armour only if it has a job

- **One collar slot.** A charm, bell, flower wreath or tag: something natural
  in this world that does not imply a human handler.
- A collar **specialises the pet's existing job**. It changes how the pet
  helps.
- **No collars of unrestricted attack, defence and loot bonuses.** That makes
  every collar the same comparison.
- **Pet armour only if pets can be harmed** and protecting them means
  something. If not, skip the slot; a cosmetic piece is worth more.
- Appearance is independent of both.

### Assignment is meaningful and forgiving

- Which horse a pet goes with is a small team decision: a Warden with the
  horse most likely to be hit, an elemental friend with a horse that sets up
  reactions.
- **No permanent bonds and no penalty for rearranging.** Free reassignment in
  town, and the current assignment always visible.
- Explain effects in plain language: "this pet helps the horse it goes
  with", never a coefficient.
- When a horse stays behind, its pet stays with it until the player moves it.

### Cuteness is part of the presentation, and is not a stat

The pet is visibly in the world and reacts to it: it follows or rides with
its horse; reacts to a victory, a rare drop, its horse being hurt; shows its
ability clearly enough to be noticed; appears beside its horse in town and in
the party view; has a name, a few animations of its own, optional
accessories.

**A player must be able to keep a favourite pet because they love it**, even
when another is more efficient.

### First version

Three pets with clearly different jobs; one pet to one horse; one collar
slot; automatic abilities with visible feedback; reassignment and collar
changes in town; naming and appearance separate from strength.

**Not in it:** pet death, breeding, complex commands, pet skill trees.

## For Horse Heroes

- This is a recommendation toward an open decision. Until she decides, a pet
  is a companion with no combat effect, which is how it is filed.
- The reactions (to a rare drop, to a victory) are loot feel. A pet that
  visibly delights in a top-tier drop is part of the fountain's aftermath,
  and is worth building before any pet ability.
- The elemental friend only makes sense once setup statuses exist
  ([elemental-combat.md](elemental-combat.md)), and its limit is the same
  bounded-effect rule that governs reactions and gear effects.
- The forager's "useful material" runs into the guardrail against extensive
  crafting. If there is nothing to craft, a forager finds something that
  already has a use, or it is not one of the first pets.
- "Keep a favourite even if another is more efficient" needs the jobs to be
  different in kind, not better and worse. If pets can be ranked, favourites
  lose. The same reasoning as signature traits for horses.
- Pet accessories and naming belong with the wardrobe
  ([cosmetics.md](cosmetics.md)), separate from the collar's effect.
- Idle-first: a pet never needs commanding.
