# The ending

> **Revised by the owner, 2026-10-10.** A restored road is free of corruption
> and bosses, and is never free of bandits and predators: there is always a
> chance of ordinary combat on any road, before and after the dragon, and
> trading goods between towns is what a finished save is for. Where this file
> says a cleared region has no combat, or that nothing is fought after the
> ending, read it as "no corrupted creatures and no boss". The newer
> statement is in [vision.md](vision.md), "The roads and the trade".

How the game ends, and what "cleared" means on the way there.

> **Success makes combat unnecessary.** You are not farming a world for ever
> after saving it; you actually save it.

The idea is the owner's: the document this comes from opens by agreeing with
her endpoint, and the rule it sets out follows from it. The specific
conditions and sequences below are the document's elaboration.

The horses travel, fight, help towns, raise families, and in the end make
themselves unnecessary as heroes, because the world is safe.

## Principles

### A region is cleared only when three things are true

| Requirement | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Corruption source removed | The local cause of the dragon's influence is defeated, cleansed, dismantled or sealed |
| Town restored | The region's settlement has reached its healthy, stable state |
| Routes secured | Every connected route in the region is safe: no road encounters and no destination boss remain |

Not "kill one boss, region complete". A region is at peace because the cause
of danger is gone, its community has recovered, and travel is truly safe.

### What clearing changes, permanently

- No ordinary monsters on its roads.
- No elite encounters and no route bosses.
- Its town is a peaceful place to shop, recruit, visit retired horses, meet
  descendants and decorate the caravan.
- Travel on cleared routes is safe, and can be summarised at once or skipped.
- Corrupted scenery gives way to restored solarpunk scenery: healthy plants,
  rebuilt bridges, communal buildings, clean water, working magical
  infrastructure, visible local life.
- On the map, the tile's danger and purity reading becomes a mark of
  completed restoration.

> Do not reintroduce monsters merely because the player wants loot. That
> would undermine the premise that the gaggle's work mattered.

### The game is finite, and that is the point

Cleared regions give no combat loot. So the player needs enough opportunity
to finish builds and collect looks **before the world runs out of danger**.

- Dangerous regions must pay meaningfully while they are dangerous.
- Uncleared regions can be revisited, with routes and destinations chosen for
  targeted farming.
- Shops, trade, restoration rewards, recruitment, pets and cosmetics give
  reasons other than combat to return to a cleared town.
- The last regions offer enough high-tier reward to prepare for the dragon
  **without infinite farming**.

The player eventually stops acquiring combat gear because there is nothing
left to fight. **That is not a flaw. It is the conclusion.**

### The dragon's defeat

It triggers the final state of the whole world:

1. Remaining corruption sources are removed or lose their power.
2. Any route still dangerous becomes safe.
3. Remaining towns finish recovering, perhaps in a time-lapse or a map
   update.
4. Retired horses, pets, residents and descendants appear in a closing
   sequence or a journal montage.
5. The map shows every region safe and restored.
6. The save is marked: "The world is safe."

The epilogue reflects this player's history: the horses in the final party;
retired companions and the towns they helped build; families and
descendants; pets adopted along the way; major restoration choices; caravan
decorations and looks tied to important places.

### What stays playable

A completed save can still be opened for peaceful things: visiting towns,
reassigning horses and pets, talking to residents, reading the family and
journey journal, decorating the caravan, using shops, viewing the restored
map, travelling safely or moving at once between towns.

Combat progression is plainly finished:

    no new corruption          no new combat loot loop
    no monster respawns        no stronger postgame bosses
    no reason to keep optimising for damage

Cosmetic collections may still be completed through peaceful trade or town
rewards, if that is supported. **Do not imply a hidden combat endgame.**

### Scope

A peaceful, open-ended mode after the ending (the idea floated was a roving
vegetable trader) is a separate, possible later expansion. **Nothing in the
first game depends on it.**

    now:     save the world -> peace -> a finite ending
    later?   save the world -> an optional peaceful trading or frontier mode

That keeps the first game honest about its promise.

## For Horse Heroes

- **This overturns two things written earlier, and both are now revised.**
  The design summary proposed that restored regions offer patrols so that
  farming never needs a place re-corrupted; under this rule a cleared region
  has no fights at all. And the retirement documents proposed an endless
  frontier of new threats after the ending; this rule says there is no combat
  after the ending, and the only postgame idea is a peaceful one, later.
- **It refines one of her travel rules.** "Every journey ends with a boss"
  holds for journeys through danger. A journey across cleared routes has no
  boss, because securing the routes is part of what clearing means.
- "A healed place must still be worth farming" becomes "a healed place must
  still be worth visiting". Its value moves from loot to people, trade, looks
  and stories.
- **It puts a hard requirement on the loot economy that no earlier file
  had.** The total combat loot in a world is bounded. Whether a player can
  assemble a party for the dragon and enjoy the wardrobe from that supply is
  something to measure in the sim, across many generated worlds, before the
  drop rates are trusted.
- It makes the order in which a player clears regions a real decision.
  Clearing a place ends its drops, so a player may reasonably leave a useful
  region dangerous a little longer. That is a choice worth showing: say
  before a region's last step that its combat loot ends with it.
- The pillar that loot feel is the product still holds, and this gives it a
  shape: the fountains are finite, so each one should count. The last
  fountain in the game is the dragon's.
- Fashion as the long game ([cosmetics.md](cosmetics.md)) fits a peaceful
  world well, and needs sources that are not combat: town rewards, gifts,
  trade, family keepsakes.
- Retirement and generations ([generations.md](generations.md)) carry on
  after the fighting stops. They are what the completed save is for.
