TUNE-LOOT-FEEL
==============
Starts on: "the drops feel off", "tune the loot shower", "more juice".
Leaves:    one changed feel parameter, seen running, with the owner's verdict.

Feel is the product (CLAUDE.md pillar 1) and it cannot be judged from code.

WHAT MAKES A DROP FEEL GOOD - the parts to keep separately tunable
  - Burst:   items leave the corpse together, fanned out, each on its own arc.
             Stagger the launches by a few frames; simultaneous reads as one.
  - Arc:     launch speed, angle spread, gravity, spin while airborne.
  - Landing: a bounce or two, a settle, a small squash, a sound on contact.
  - Rarity:  colour, a light beam for the top tiers, a distinct sound per
             tier. The rare drop must be recognisable before it lands.
  - Pickup:  a pause on the ground, then a vacuum toward the hero that
             accelerates; a tick per item, pitch rising across a stream.
  - Response: screen shake and a flash sized to the best item in the burst,
             not to the count.
  - Volume:  a big kill must hold up with dozens of items at once, both in
             frame time and in legibility.

STEPS
1. Keep every feel number in one resource (or one data file) with a name, not
   scattered as literals. If it is not there yet, move it there first.
2. Make the scene repeatable: a debug key or startup flag that spawns a fixed
   burst from a fixed seed, so two runs can be compared.
3. Change ONE parameter. Run it. Look at it (build-and-run.txt step 4).
4. Show the owner and ask, with the tool, offering the before, the after, and
   a step further in the same direction.
5. Write the value she picks into the defaults and commit.

DO NOT
  - Tune several parameters in one go; nobody can say which one helped.
  - Call a feel change done on the strength of a build.
